8. Cooling-Fan Monitoring

The SD-8400 compares each commanded fan-speed signal with the corresponding feedback signal and records a derived variance for equipment-health diagnostics.

8.1 Interpreting command and feedback

SignalMeaningInterpretation limit
Fan commandController-requested fan speed.Does not prove mechanical rotation.
Fan feedbackReported speed from the fan-feedback circuit.May be affected by fan, connector, wiring, or sensing conditions.
Derived varianceAbsolute difference between command and feedback.Identifies disagreement; it does not identify the failed component.

8.2 Authorized response boundary

  • Use site-approved procedures before inspecting or servicing cooling equipment.
  • Do not bypass fan supervision, force a fan command, or clear a maintenance hold solely to restore output.
  • Opening an enclosure or contacting internal components requires the applicable electrical isolation and lockout/tagout process.
Safety note: This synthetic manual does not replace site electrical-safety, isolation, or lockout/tagout requirements.

8.4 Fault A12 — Cooling Fan Feedback Variance

Fault A12 is asserted when the derived cooling-fan command/feedback variance exceeds the configured 12-percentage-point threshold during the controller diagnostic window. The inverter enters Maintenance Hold so the cooling system can be inspected before return to service.

Expected behavior: A12 establishes that commanded and reported fan speed disagreed beyond the configured limit. It does not, by itself, prove a failed fan. Obstruction, connector or wiring conditions, feedback sensing, and intermittent mechanical drag remain possible until inspected.

Required checks

  1. Keep the inverter in Maintenance Hold and confirm the event and diagnostic window with the control room.
  2. Inspect accessible intake screens, exhaust openings, fan guards, and the exterior cooling-air path for obstruction, contamination, or damage.
  3. Compare fan command and fan feedback. Treat missing, unstable, or persistently divergent feedback as unresolved.
  4. Use the approved site procedure before any internal inspection. Do not open energized compartments for this check.
  5. Apply the approved return-to-service checklist after corrective work; do not clear A12 solely because the alarm is acknowledged.
Evidence interpretation: High ambient temperature can increase fan demand, but ambient temperature alone does not explain excessive command/feedback variance or establish component failure.
